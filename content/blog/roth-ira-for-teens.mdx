If you have a job, a Roth IRA may be one of the few places where being young is
a real advantage. This guide explains how it works in general. It is not tax or
legal advice, and the rules have details and limits that change, so check
[IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov) and talk to a parent, guardian, or qualified
professional before you act.

## What a Roth IRA is

An IRA is an **individual retirement account**. In a Roth IRA you put in money
you have already paid income tax on. The money then grows inside the account, and
**qualified withdrawals in retirement are not taxed**. That tax treatment is the
whole point.

An IRA is just a container. What you buy inside it, such as index funds, is a
separate choice.

## The earned-income rule

You can only contribute to an IRA if you have **earned income**, meaning money you
made from working, such as a paycheck or self-employment income. Gifts,
allowance, and investment income do not count. Your contribution cannot be more
than what you earned that year, and there is also a yearly maximum set by the
IRS.

## Can a minor open one?

Generally a minor cannot open an investment account alone. A parent or guardian
opens a **custodial Roth IRA**, which is managed by the adult until the young
person reaches the age where they take over, which depends on the state. The
money belongs to the young person.

## Why starting young is powerful

Tax-free growth plus decades of compounding is a strong combination. The longer
the money stays invested, the more of the final balance comes from growth. You can
see the effect by running the numbers in
[this compound interest example](/blog/compound-interest-python).

## Rules to know before you start

- **Withdrawals:** you can take out the amount you originally *contributed* at any
  time without tax or penalty, but earnings generally must stay until you are
  older (59½ is the usual age) and the account has been open long enough, or you
  may owe tax and a penalty. Exceptions exist.
- **It is long-term money.** Do not put in money you may need soon.
- **Investments can lose value.** The account does not guarantee anything.
- **Beware of sales pitches.** Anyone promising a guaranteed return is a red
  flag.

## What to do next

1. Check whether you have earned income this year and keep your pay records.
2. Talk to a parent or guardian about a custodial account.
3. Read the IRS information on IRAs, and the investor education pages at
   [investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov).
