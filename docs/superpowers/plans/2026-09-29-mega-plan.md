## 1. The bet, on one page

**What:** StrikeLab gives ambitious high-school clubs a ready-to-run technical-finance lab where students learn by coding real market models (option pricing, backtests, risk) and finish with work they can show.

**Who:**

| Role | Who they are |
|---|---|
| **User** | Students aged 13–18 in math, CS, investing, economics, DECA/FBLA and competition clubs |
| **Distributor** | The club leader or teacher, who launches a cohort and keeps students accountable |
| **First payer** | The club or school |
| **Later payers** | Districts, summer programs, and sponsors (§8) |

**Why it can win:**
1. **The product is the work.** Students implement Black-Scholes and backtests in a browser with nothing to install. Simulators teach clicking and financial-literacy curricula teach vocabulary, while StrikeLab produces inspectable code and a capstone. That's a stronger college-application artifact than a certificate, and a stronger learning signal for teachers.
2. **Zero-prep for leaders.** A six-week template, weekly plans, copy-ready messages and a scorecard. Leader time is the scarce resource in every club, and StrikeLab spends almost none of it.
3. **Distribution through leaders.** One leader brings 10–25 students, and leaders talk to other leaders. The expensive part of edtech (reaching students) goes through someone who already has them.

**What must be true** (and the evidence that will tell us):

| Assumption | Evidence that confirms it | By |
|---|---|---|
| Leaders will run it | ≥ 2 fall pilots locked by Oct 16, 5 cohorts by Jan 31 | Gates G1, G4 |
| Students activate and stay | ≥ 60% activation, ≥ 40% week-4 retention | G3 (W14) |
| The capstone is the hook | ≥ 35% of activated students submit one; leaders cite it | G3, G4 |
| Leaders come back and refer | ≥ 4 of 5 would run it again; ≥ 3 referrals | G4 (W24) |
| Someone pays | ≥ 2 orgs pay or sign a dated, priced commitment | G5 (W28) |
| It scales past the founder | Support hours per cohort fall with each cohort; a leader runs a cohort without the founder present | G5, G6 |

**What this is not:** a trading app, a stock-picking game, a finance-literacy course, or a marketplace. Minors' data is private by default, forever.

## 3. North star and metric tree

**North star:** *students who submit a capstone in a leader-run cohort.* It can't be gamed without doing the real thing. It requires a leader (distribution), activation, retention and the capstone (value). It's also the number a YC partner, a principal and a parent all understand.

```
Revenue (ARR, paying orgs)
└── Paying orgs ← leader reuse × price acceptance × buyer reach (§8)
    └── Leader reuse ← outcomes visible (scorecard) × low burden (support hrs/cohort)
        └── ★ Capstones submitted in leader-run cohorts
            ├── Cohorts started ← pipeline: contacts → calls → locked pilots (§9)
            ├── Activation (≥60%) ← kickoff in the room, sign-up that works, first session
            ├── Week-4 retention (≥40%) ← schedule fit, weekly nudges, cohort home
            └── Capstone rate (≥35% of activated) ← synced code, examples, briefing
```

## 8. Money: the revenue ladder

List prices stay as stated from day 1: **$199/yr per independent club, $499/yr per school instructor**, and pilots free. At these prices, club and school licenses are a **wedge, not the business**: 100 paying orgs at a ~$300 blended price is only about $30k ARR. The plan is honest about that, and each rung is unlocked by evidence from the one below:

| Rung | Buyer | Price shape (assumption) | Unlocks when | Why it could be big |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Club and school licenses | Club budget, school department | $199 / $499 per year | G5 | It proves willingness to pay, and it's the distribution engine |
| 2. District agreements | District CTE / advanced-academics budgets | $3k–$20k per year per district | ≥ 3 schools in one district paying; a DPA template signed | Deal sizes of 10–50× with the same product and a district view (Q28) |
| 3. Summer and after-school cohorts | Parents | $150–$400 per student per cohort | G5, plus ≥ 20 families asking | Reaches students without a club; unit economics are set by TA cost |
| 4. Sponsored seats | Finance firms, prop shops, alumni foundations, local banks | $5k–$50k per sponsor per year, funding N clubs | ≥ 100 capstones and anonymous outcome data | Sponsors pay for reach into schools that lack quant exposure (the mission line); schools pay nothing |
| 5. Talent pipeline | Employers, internship programs | Later: per-program or per-placement | ≥ 1,000 capstones, and students opt in | The long-term moat is a verified record of what students built |

**Unit economics to measure from the first paid org:**
- founder or support hours per org per term (target < 2h);
- the conversion rate from free pilot to paid;
- the renewal rate;
- hosting cost per student (Vercel plus Supabase: close to $0 at pilot scale; watch Supabase and Vercel limits once past the free tiers).

**Pricing experiments, in order, and only after G4:**
1. State the price in the first call (already done).
2. Offer a 2-term price.
3. Offer the district price to schools with 3+ interested teachers.

Never discount below list for the first 10 customers. It muddies the proof.

## 10. Company, legal and the minor-founder checklist

*Not legal advice. Every item marked ⚖ needs a qualified person (a startup lawyer, a law-school clinic, or a parent's professional contact) before it's relied on.*

The founder is a high-school student. That's an asset with teachers and investors, but it changes some mechanics. Many agreements signed by a minor can be voided by the minor, so counterparties (schools, Stripe, banks, investors) often require an adult to sign or own the account.

| # | Item | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Who signs pilot agreements and DPAs** | Before the first path-B/C pilot | Often a parent or guardian as an authorized signatory, or the school's own template signed by the leader's school. ⚖ |
| L2 | **Stripe account ownership** | Before the first payment | Stripe requires the account representative to be an adult; typically a parent is the representative until the founder is 18. ⚖ Confirm current Stripe rules |
| L3 | **Company formation** | Before the first paid contract or any investment | A Delaware C-corp is the usual shape for venture-backed companies (YC's standard). Formation, officers, the founder's stock and vesting, and 83(b) timing need an adult incorporator/officer and a lawyer. ⚖ |
| L4 | **IP assignment** | With L3 | All code, content and brand assigned to the company, including anything written before formation. ⚖ |
| L5 | **Bank account** | With L3 | An adult co-signer is usually required. |
| L6 | **Privacy policy, terms, pilot agreement and DPA template reviewed** | Before the first paid contract (master plan E7) | FERPA applies to the schools; StrikeLab acts as a service provider under the school's direction. State student-privacy laws vary. Prefer signing the school's standard DPA. ⚖ |
| L7 | **Testimonial and case-study consent** (student + guardian + leader) | Before any quote or capstone goes public (E8) | Template in `docs/gtm/` when first needed |
| L8 | **Insurance** | Only if a district contract requires it | General or cyber liability; quotes vary. ⚖ |
| L9 | **Trademark search for "StrikeLab"** | Before spending on brand | A free USPTO search first |

**Budget reality:** L3–L6 are the only items likely to cost real money. Law-school clinics and startup-program legal partners can cut the cost substantially. **Don't form the company until L3's trigger fires**, but decide who the adult signatory is **this month** (L1), because the fall pilots may need it.

## 12. Risks and kill criteria

The master plan §8 register still applies. The risks that decide the company:

| Risk | Signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Leaders won't commit | < 3 calls by Oct 2; no yes by Oct 16 | Plan B; change the segment or pitch at G3 |
| Students don't come back | Week-4 < 40% in 2 cohorts | Reminders (Q15), pacing changes, a shorter program (4 weeks) as an experiment |
| The capstone doesn't land | < 20% submit | Two fixed prompts plus a worked example; the capstone starts in week 4, not 6 |
| It only works with the founder in the room | Leader-run cohorts underperform founder-supported ones by > 20 points | Invest in `burden` items and the in-app facilitator guide before scaling |
| Nobody pays | G5 fails | The buyer ladder (§8), in order; not more features |
| The founder runs out of hours | < 8 hours for 2 weeks in a row | Cut product work first; cut to 1 pilot per term |
| A privacy incident with minors | Any | The incident runbook; transparency with the leader within 24 hours; pause the feature |

**Kill or pivot criteria**, written now so they're honest later:

| If … | Then |
|---|---|
| By Apr 25, fewer than 3 cohorts have ever run despite ≥ 100 qualified contacts | The club-leader channel doesn't work. Pivot the distribution to summer programs or direct-to-student, or treat StrikeLab as a portfolio project rather than a company |
| 5 cohorts ran but activation or retention stayed under half the thresholds after two fix cycles | The product doesn't hold students. Rethink the format before any more distribution |
| Strong outcomes but $0 after trying three buyers | Run it as a free, sponsor- or grant-funded nonprofit program, which is a legitimate outcome for the mission |
