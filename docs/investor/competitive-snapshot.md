# Competitive positioning snapshot

**Researched:** October 9, 2026. This is a founder research note for buyer interviews and investor diligence, not a claim that StrikeLab wins a head-to-head evaluation. Product descriptions and prices are from the organizations' own pages and should be rechecked before a deck is sent.

## Landscape

| Alternative | What its own materials emphasize | Buyer strength to respect | StrikeLab question to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| [HowTheMarketWorks](https://www.howthemarketworks.com/) | Free classroom stock market game, built-in lessons, contest setup, teacher reports, live market prices | A leader can run a free investing activity with familiar competition mechanics | Does a leader value student-written models and capstones enough to choose a different format and eventually pay? |
| [PersonalFinanceLab](https://www.personalfinancelab.com/pricing/) | Budget and stock games plus learning materials; public starting prices of $10 per student for one game or $15 for both, with volume discounts | Broad financial-literacy content, reporting, and school purchasing options | Is the code-first technical-finance outcome a distinct budget item for a club or instructor? |
| [Wharton Global High School Investment Competition](https://globalyouth.wharton.upenn.edu/competitions/) | Free, educator-guided team investment competition and simulator with strategy work | Strong brand, team motivation, and a concrete event | Do leaders want a repeatable weekly coding lab outside a competition season? Do not market StrikeLab as preparation for this competition: [its rules restrict outside courses that claim to teach it](https://globalyouth.wharton.upenn.edu/competitions/investment-competition/rules-roles/). |
| [QuantConnect Quant League](https://www.quantconnect.com/announcements/17502/introducing-quant-league/p0) | Algorithmic-trading competition, student strategy portfolio, employer visibility; its announcement describes university teams already strong in Python | Serious code and career signaling for advanced students | Can StrikeLab become an easier high-school entry point without claiming the same competition or recruiting outcomes? |

## Positioning hypothesis

StrikeLab's proposed narrow position is a **six-week, leader-ready technical-finance lab for high-school clubs, with browser coding and inspectable student work**. This is a description of the offer in the repo, not validated market differentiation. The closest free alternatives already offer teacher reports, curriculum, and simulations, so “teacher dashboard” and “stock game” are weak stand-alone differentiators. The candidate difference to test is whether a leader wants each student to produce and explain code, with low setup effort and a paced program.

## Buyer interview prompts

1. Which of these alternatives have you actually used, evaluated, or heard students request? What did you choose and why?
2. If a free stock game already handles meetings, what would make a code-first lab worth a new approval step?
3. How many students can complete a coding capstone with your current meeting time and device access?
4. Who can pay $199 for a club or $499 for an instructor, and what existing purchase replaces or complements it?
5. What evidence would convince you to run this again: attendance, completed work, student explanations, teacher time saved, or something else?

## Action from this review

- In discovery calls, ask which alternative the leader currently uses before showing StrikeLab. Record the answer in the private CRM.
- Test the technical-work outcome and low-prep claim with an outside leader; do not assert they are unique until buyers confirm the difference matters.
- Keep a dated source link for any public competitor price or feature claim. Avoid competitor usage figures unless independently verified.
- Do not pitch the Wharton competition as an integration, partner, or permitted preparation route.
